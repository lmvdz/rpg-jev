# Three experimentation journeys: heat and a covering

## Status and purpose

This is a **design-only worked proposal** for the accepted
[experimentation learning milestone](experimentation.md#first-milestone-learn-a-rule-and-use-it-creatively).
It is not an implemented encounter, an admitted thermal/measurement mechanism,
a measured result, or a change to Q018. Q060 still needs learning evidence.

**Sequencing update:** the [common interaction-contract review](interaction-contract.md)
comes first. These journeys are preserved for later evaluation, not selected as
the foundation or a required first domain. Revise or replace them after the
cross-domain representation and mechanism review.

Heat, contact and insulation remain a candidate first domain. These journeys ask
whether one small family of mechanisms could support a correct hypothesis, an
informative mistake and transfer to an unfamiliar problem. They are alternative
player paths around a shared apparatus, not a script requiring one person to
hold contradictory beliefs in sequence.

The proposed learning target is:

> A covering can change how quickly heat is exchanged. It does not necessarily
> add heat, and the useful arrangement depends on which side is warmer.

This statement must ultimately follow from admitted material properties,
structure, contacts and boundary conditions, not a rule named "insulating wrap."
The same code must determine both warming and cooling.

## Shared apparatus and evidence rules

Use comparable solid blocks, removable dry passive coverings and a stable room
environment for a bounded observation interval. Initial stored heat is finite;
no combustion, powered heater, phase change, liquid, fracture or seasonal ecology
is needed for this proposal. Initial conditions and environmental exchanges are
accounted, not a free source hidden in a fixture.

The player can inspect the arrangement, propose placing/removing a covering, wait
for a chosen interval and compare accessible temperature observations. Names,
art and dialogue must not label the answer "heat generator" or "correct insulator."
The experiment's explanation is withheld until the player's prediction and
interpretation have been recorded.

### Information available to the player

- Which objects are comparable, their visible covering/contact arrangement and
  any preparation steps the player actually observed.
- The initial and later readings produced by a proposed accessible measuring
  instrument, with stated resolution, uncertainty and response delay.
- Elapsed observation time and relevant known room conditions.
- A record of earlier observations, not a privileged dump of all part temperatures.

**Measurement is an unresolved prerequisite, not a free ability.** A candidate is
a probe at a consistent accessible site thermally coupled to each block. The
physical design must explain that access and the probe's loading/contact path;
it cannot read a hidden core through an otherwise closed covering by fiat.
Whether the first study uses calibrated numeric readings, reliable qualitative
comparisons or another instrument remains open. Internal quantities follow
accepted SI conventions; presentation units need not be kelvin.

### What the proposed fixture must control

Match block material, mass, shape, initial thermal state, measurement site,
exposure time and room boundary. Record covering geometry, material, initial
state and contact arrangement rather than treating "covered" as a free flag.
For a strong comparison, include the same covering material in the reference
setup but positioned away from the block, so arrangement rather than resource
creation is the intervention. Record any residual environmental coupling.

Do not assume matching initial core readings means matching the whole assembly's
energy distribution. The covering and probe have heat capacity, may draw heat
initially, and may alter exposed area or contact resistance. The designer must
admit and validate the full comparison before calling its result informative.
The player is testing an arrangement's effective response, not uniquely measuring
an intrinsic conductivity from one trial.

A signal must exceed both instrument uncertainty and the solver's admitted error
over the chosen interval. Too small a difference is an **inconclusive observation**,
not player failure or permission to exaggerate the evidence. No temperatures,
coefficients, durations or study success rates are invented in this draft.

## Journey 1: a correct hypothesis

### Player question

> "Can I make this warm block stay warm longer without supplying more heat?"

The player has two comparable warm blocks and the passive covering. They observe
their starting readings and know there is no continuing heater. They propose:

> "Putting the covering around one block will slow its cooling."

### Intervention and trace

| Step | Player action or evidence | World requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Predict | State which block should cool more slowly, and why | Record the prediction before displaying the result |
| Arrange | Cover one block; leave the reference uncovered with its covering material nearby | Change actual interfaces/exposure without adding material or resetting temperatures |
| Compare | Read both blocks at the same elapsed intervals and accessible sites | Advance the same admitted thermal mechanisms, including covering/probe/boundary exchange |
| Interpret | Compare each change from its measured starting state | Expose observations, not a verdict or hidden causal diagnosis |
| Challenge | Repeat with fresh comparable prepared conditions, swapping sides or object labels | No reload reroll, ignored preparation cost or label-selected behaviour |

**Expected evidence, conditional on fixture validation:** after the declared
transient/comparison window, the covered block loses less temperature than the
reference by an observable margin. Both still exchange heat; an early transient
may differ. The engine must not force this ordering for every arbitrary covering,
geometry or duration.

**Conclusion supported:** this arrangement slowed cooling under the tested
conditions. It does not prove that every covering works, that heat has been
created, or that the covered block will stay warm indefinitely.

**Persistent consequence:** each block and covering retains its resulting state;
the player's measurement record can support a later comparison. A retry needs
new accounted preparation, not a silently restored pristine object.

**What would make the journey fail as a design:** only a named blanket rule
produces the difference; the covering has no physical interface; readouts reveal
privileged state; or indistinguishable measurements are narrated as a discovery.

## Journey 2: a plausible wrong hypothesis

### Player question

A player sees the covered warm block retain a higher reading and concludes:

> "The covering makes heat. I could warm a cold object just by wrapping it."

This is a plausible explanation of the first comparison, not stupidity and not
an interpretation the game must assign to the player. The study records whether
someone actually proposes it; the branch remains a diagnostic case otherwise.

### Discriminating experiment

The player proposes a new comparison with both complete setups initially at the
room's temperature: blocks, coverings and probes have no relevant temperature
difference or admitted active heat-producing process. One covering surrounds a
block; the reference covering stays beside its block.

Before the trial, record whether they predict detectable warming above the
reference/room in these exact equilibrium conditions. If they do not, this trial
does not contradict their hypothesis; do not force them into that prediction.
Then observe both over the same interval. Preparation and handling are explicit:
sustained hand contact, mechanical work, sunlight or another source would confound
the claim and must be absent, bounded or accounted. Do not pretend these inputs
are zero merely because the player did not notice them.

**Expected evidence under those declared conditions:** no measurable rise above
the comparison baseline attributable to the passive covering. This is a supported
near-equilibrium result, not an engine message that a missing mechanism did
nothing. The earlier warm-block comparison is then available beside it.

**Revision the evidence supports:**

> "It did not warm the block by itself in this test. Slowing the loss of stored
> heat remains a supported explanation of the first result."

The result contradicts the recorded prediction of detectable autonomous heating
at room equilibrium, not every possible heat-generation explanation. It does
not uniquely exclude temperature-dependent generation or redistribution as an
observer's alternative explanation of the earlier result. Returning to warm
initial conditions helps distinguish a working interaction from an inert prop
whose mechanisms simply were not implemented; further discrimination requires
another supported experiment, not an omniscient verdict.

Do not substitute "wrap a cold block and notice it warms" as the decisive test:
a cold block can gain heat from warmer surroundings without the covering
generating any. Ambient exchange is part of the causal account.

**Persistent consequence:** the player gains a constrained negative result and
can revise their hypothesis. No mandatory reward item, damage or social subplot
is needed to make the information useful.

**What would make the journey fail:** an inconclusive reading is treated as
disproof; unsupported wrapping is mislabeled a physical no-effect; an unreported
source explains warming; or an omniscient explanation replaces investigation.

## Journey 3: an unfamiliar application

### Player goal

Later, the player must preserve a **cold solid reference piece** during a waiting
or delivery interval in warmer surroundings. Its usefulness depends on its
measured condition on arrival. No ice melting, spoilage or biological injury
model is implied; the exact task criterion and interval remain to be chosen.

The available materials include the same passive covering among other ordinary
objects. The task does not say "use insulation" or repeat the warm-block setup.
The player can inspect the initial conditions and proposes:

> "If that arrangement slowed heat leaving a warm object, it might also slow
> heat entering this cold one."

### Intervention and trace

- Record the prediction before use: what should change relative to an uncovered
  reference, what should stay the same, and which contact/exposure is responsible?
- Let the player arrange the covering, change supported contact/exposure, choose
  a shorter interval or devise another supported approach. Do not require a
  canonical crafting recipe or score only the word "wrap."
- Compare against a separately prepared uncovered reference with matched material,
  starting state, exposure interval and observation conditions. A delivery variant
  must also account for carrier contact and route conditions; otherwise use a
  stationary interval first.
- Record the arriving/ending observation and the player's explanation before
  giving feedback. Both cold pieces may warm; the useful prediction is a slower
  change, not freezing temperature forever.

**Expected evidence, conditional on the admitted arrangement:** the covered
reference warms more slowly over the tested interval, without a negative-heat
source or a special "keep cold" rule. Initial covering temperature and probe/contact
effects still matter; the task is not guaranteed by selecting any covering.

**Near-transfer evidence:** the player recognizes the reversed temperature
difference, predicts the direction of exchange, and deliberately changes a
relevant path in a context that did not instruct them to repeat the first answer.
Simply copying the previous gesture, guessing correctly, or already knowing
insulation from real life does not establish new learning.
Passing this candidate alone does not establish Q063's creative unfamiliar
application: a memorized "wrap it" response can still succeed.

Ask for a prediction in a further variant: the covering is beside rather than
around the piece, or an admitted bypass contact remains exposed. Only use a
variant whose physics and cues are supported. This helps distinguish understanding
of the path from believing that owning the same item grants a temperature bonus,
but still need not distinguish it from the recipe "wrap anything."

A stronger creative-transfer claim needs an unprompted, path-sensitive application:
for example, the cold piece rests on a warmer conducting support that bypasses
its covering. Present the observable arrangement and goal, not "remove the bypass."
Ask the player to propose a supported change, predict its effect and explain
which exchange path changes. Separating the contact or placing suitable material
at that interface can be candidate interventions if admitted; wrapping unrelated
exposed surfaces is not scripted to solve it. Record a counterfactual prediction
as well as the achieved condition. Even success here must be compared with
initial familiarity before attributing learning to the game.

**What would make the journey fail:** the engine special-cases the task label,
the UI announces the answer, all strategies are scripted to succeed, or the
study attributes pre-existing player knowledge to the game.

## Shared causal requirement and approximation counterexample

The journeys use one proposed mechanism family in different conditions:

**stored thermal state + material properties + explicit interfaces/exposure +
elapsed time → accounted exchanges → accessible observations.**

Removing the original source does not remove stored energy. A lower-exchange
path does not produce energy. Reversing a temperature difference should be
resolved by the same admitted exchange mechanism, not a second recipe.

An adversarial representation pair contains the same warm block, covering
material and initial quantities: in one arrangement the covering surrounds the
block; in the other it is beside it. Equal aggregate mass/energy does not make
the enabled boundary paths equivalent. If an approximation stores only those
totals and cannot distinguish the proposed observations, it is insufficient for
this study. Stored structure must predate evolution; it cannot be invented after
the player predicts an outcome.

### Geometry questions exposed by the journeys

The subsequently accepted
[hierarchical frames and frame-change invariant](composition-and-processes.md#accepted-q018-coordinates-hierarchical-frames-and-invariant-changes)
remain binding. These are requirements to compare candidate geometry under Q018,
not a selected shape palette or coordinate schema:

- A block needs enough stored extent and exposed surface/interface information
  to derive its admitted thermal approximation, not only an assembly label.
- A covering needs meaningful thickness, coverage and interfaces on its sides;
  a gap or bypass cannot vanish into a whole-object "insulated" flag.
- A probe needs a supported accessible contact site and accounted loading; a
  decorative probe mesh does not create a measurement capability.
- The stronger transfer variant needs a distinct support contact the player
  can inspect and potentially alter, not a named task-specific exception.
- Re-expressing either setup in different region/local/part frames must not
  alter its result. Moving it to a genuinely warmer room can. Addressing cells
  are not thermal barriers or permission to settle an interface twice.

Simple solids plus explicit layers/interfaces may suffice; these examples do
not establish support for arbitrary cavities, deformation, fluids or fracture.
The shape palette, contact approximation and precision must be assessed together
with the measurement/error requirements, rather than chosen from visual appeal.

Before a future playtest, validate and freeze the physical fixture and measurement
conditions independently of player predictions. Do not tune a result after seeing
which hypothesis the player offers. A physically non-diagnostic fixture must be
reworked or reported as such, not rescued by explanatory prose.

Capture initial familiarity, predictions and reasons, interventions, elapsed
conditions, observations, hypothesis revisions, unfamiliar applications and
confounding cues. Compare performance before and after the learning opportunity;
baseline knowledge and cue-only success must be distinguished from acquired
transfer. Participant count, comparison design, scoring and pass threshold remain
open. Proposed outcomes in this document are not collected evidence.

After Q064's cross-domain review, any use of these journeys still requires choosing
the observation method and an admitted mechanism subset, then testing alternatives.
The [representation comparison](representation-options.md) preserves three
physical-domain candidates and their consequences for covered, uncovered and
bypass-contact setups; it does not select the common interaction foundation.
This draft does not authorize implementation or broaden the study into every
mechanism mentioned by the earlier agent reports. Major renderer design remains
deferred; no particular interface, art style or camera is selected.
