# World bible: one night at the Gilded Carp

One page. Every question, template and schedule in the inn is written against it. If a line of prose or a judge question contradicts this page, this page wins.

## Setting

The Gilded Carp is a roadside inn on the river road, two days from any town. It is late autumn, it has rained since noon, and the ford is too high to cross, so whoever is here tonight stays here tonight. The rooms that matter are the **common room** (bar, hearth, the stairs), the **kitchen** behind it, the **cellar** under the kitchen (locked; the iron key hangs in the kitchen), the **office** off the common room (locked; Mara carries the brass key, a spare hangs beside the iron one), and the **stable yard** outside.

The crown's assessor arrives at first light to read the inn's account ledger and set its tax. An inn that cannot show its ledger is assessed at the assessor's pleasure, which would finish the Carp. This afternoon the ledger vanished from the office.

## Tone

Low and close: wet wool, tallow light, people who have known each other too long. No magic, no prophecy, no chosen one. Stakes are a livelihood, a job, a debt. People speak plainly and briefly; nobody explains themselves unless pressed. Humour is dry and comes from character. Violence is possible, ugly, and makes everything worse.

## The player

A stranger who came in out of the rain this afternoon and took a bed. Nobody here owes them anything. They were in the common room at dusk, which is near the office door, which is enough.

## The three

**Mara Venn, innkeeper and owner.** *Cautious, fair-minded, proud, behind on her debts.* She built this place and will not be made a fool of in it. She does not like accusing a guest, but someone took her ledger and a stranger is the easy answer. She trusts Tobin's word and Odo's cooking, in that order, though she has never had cause to doubt Odo's word either.
*Wants tonight:* the ledger back before dawn. Failing that, someone to answer for it.
*Starts out believing:* the stranger took the ledger (likely, not certain), because Odo told her he saw the stranger by the office door at dusk.
*Will do, left alone:* demand to search the stranger's pack mid-evening; near midnight, decide what to do with them.

**Tobin Reed, stablehand.** *Quiet, observant, timid, loyal to Mara.* He notices everything and says almost none of it. He owes Odo a season's wages, borrowed for his sister's medicine, and Odo reminds him of it kindly and often.
*Wants tonight:* to keep his place, keep out of it, and not to be the reason the Carp closes.
*Knows:* at dusk he saw Odo carry something wrapped in oilcloth down the cellar steps. He has told no one.
*Will do, left alone:* wrestle with it around ten o'clock, and most likely say nothing.

**Odo Brask, cook.** *Genial, greedy, quick-tongued, embellishes every story.* He has skimmed from the kitchen purchases for two years to pay river gamblers, and the ledger would show it to anyone who reads it carefully, which an assessor will. He took the ledger this afternoon with a kitchen knife through the office latch, wrapped it in his spare apron and an oilcloth, and buried it in the flour barrel in the cellar. He means to burn it once the house is asleep.
*Wants tonight:* the blame to sit on the stranger until the ledger is ash.
*Will do, left alone:* tell anyone who listens what he "saw"; check the cellar; burn the ledger late in the evening.

## What is true, and who knows it

| Fact | Known to |
| --- | --- |
| Odo took the ledger and hid it in the cellar flour barrel | Odo |
| Odo carried an oilcloth bundle to the cellar at dusk | Tobin (saw it), Odo |
| The stranger was by the office door at dusk | Nobody: it is Odo's lie. Mara holds it as likely |
| Odo owes money to river gamblers; his markers are in his coat in the kitchen | Odo |
| Tobin owes Odo a season's wages | Tobin, Odo |
| The office latch was forced with a thin blade, and there is flour on the sill | Whoever looks |

## How the night can go

The quest is one sentence: **Mara stops believing the stranger took her ledger.** Nothing in the code says how. Routes we expect to work: putting real evidence in front of her (the ledger in Odo's apron, the markers, the floured sill); getting Tobin, whom she trusts, to say what he saw; making Odo give himself away in front of her. Routes we expect to fail, or to cost something: bare denial, threats, handing back the ledger with no account of where it was. A second guard decides whether she also knows who did it, which changes the ending.

Talk travels. What Tobin sees he may mention to Odo over supper; what Odo hears he improves. By mid-evening Mara may hold a version of the player's doings that the player would not recognise, and she will say so, naming who told her.

## Voice

- **Mara:** short sentences, dry, direct address. "You were by my office door. Don't tell me about the rain."
- **Tobin:** halting, trails off, looks elsewhere. "I don't... it's not my place to say."
- **Odo:** warm, wordy, a joke before the knife. "Friend! Sit, eat. Terrible business about the ledger, terrible. They say you were near it?"
